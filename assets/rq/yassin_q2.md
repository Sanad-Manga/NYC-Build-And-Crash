Which boroughs account for a larger share of collisions than of construction, and has that changed?

Using new buildings, Manhattan has the largest mean positive gap in full years 2022–2025 (+14.78 percentage points), and Staten Island the largest negative gap (-24.39 points). Bronx: +4.98 points in 2022, -2.73 in 2025, -1.34 in partial January–October 2026. Brooklyn: +9.69 points in 2022, +10.51 in 2025, +10.44 in partial January–October 2026. Manhattan: +13.52 points in 2022, +14.90 in 2025, +15.03 in partial January–October 2026. Queens: -4.93 points in 2022, -8.61 in 2025, -4.92 in partial January–October 2026. Staten Island: -23.27 points in 2022, -14.07 in 2025, -19.21 in partial January–October 2026. Positive means a larger collision share; negative a larger new-building share. These are shares of retained ZIP-coded activity, not all city crashes, and 2026 is not directly comparable to a full year because of seasonality. About 28% of upstream crashes have no usable ZIP and are excluded, especially in 2022–2023. There is no traffic or population exposure denominator; deaths per collision describe severity among reported crashes, not a resident's risk. ZIPs are not neighborhoods. Busy areas can have both more construction and traffic. Coverage, composition and reporting can confound these associations; interval overlap is not a formal test of a group difference.
